A calendar heatmap with the events that explain it. Each day is coloured by your measure, and promotions, campaigns and holidays sit on the day they happened. Supported Power BI features: cross-filtering by day and by date range, Ctrl+click multi-select, highlighting · bookmarks · tooltips and report tooltip pages · context menu · custom color rules · format on the canvas (on-object) · setup guide inside the visual · keyboard navigation and screen-reader labels · high contrast · number formats from the model · 12 languages. Calendar: year, month and week views with drill up and down, ISO or US week numbers, fiscal calendars by month or in 4-4-5 weeks, 10 color schemes, 5 style templates, a color legend and measure switching.

Quick Start

1. Add the visual to your report

In Power BI Desktop, go to the Visualizations pane → click the three dots → Get more visuals → search for Calendar Events Heatmap.

2. Add a date and a measure

Drag a Date column to Date and a measure to Main Measure. The heatmap appears at once. The automatic date hierarchy (Year, Quarter, Month, Day) works too: the visual rebuilds each date from its levels.

3. Follow the setup guide

While you edit, a small panel inside the visual lists the optional fields — events, categories, holidays, more measures — ticks each one as you add it, and lets you pick a style template with one click. Close it with ✕ or Finish; reopen it from Format → Style template → Setup guide. It never appears in reading view.

Views and navigation

  • Month — one month, or up to 12 side by side (Pro). Months to Display is a maximum: only the months that fit the width are drawn.
  • Year (Pro) — the twelve months in miniature, each day coloured. Click a month name to open it.
  • Week (Pro) — seven large days with every event written out.
  • Drill up and down: the title goes up a level (week → month → year); a week number opens that week; Today jumps to the current date; ← → move by month, week, year or fiscal period.
  • Week numbers (Calendar → Week Numbers): ISO 8601 (Monday weeks, week 1 holds the first Thursday) or US (Sunday weeks, week 1 holds 1 January).

Selecting and filtering

  • Click a day to filter the page; Ctrl+click adds; click empty space to clear.
  • A date range: drag across days, or click one day and Shift+click another — every day in between filters the report. With the keyboard, Shift+Enter selects from the last chosen day to the focused one.
  • Selected days are outlined and the rest dimmed; bookmarks restore the selection; Edit interactions is respected; right-click opens the Power BI context menu.

Colour: schemes, rules, templates

  • Color scheme (Heatmap Colors): Blues, Greens, Purples, Greys, Red-yellow-green and its reverse, Viridis and Cividis (colour-blind safe), Activity (GitHub style), TCViz blue — or Custom, with your own minimum and maximum colours.
  • Rows of the same day (Heatmap Colors): Automatic (the default) counts the day's value once when every row of the day repeats it — sales and events in two tables related by date, or merged in Power Query — and adds the rows when each one carries its own share, as in the sample data. Sum, Average or Maximum force one rule.
  • Color Rules: up to three rules — greater than or equal to, or less than or equal to a value — each with its own colour. The first rule that matches paints the day, over the gradient. They are the visual's conditional formatting: Power BI does not offer its fx button to visuals that read data as a table, which this one does.
  • Style template: Classic (the default look), Clean, Tiles, Minimal and TCViz. A template only fills what you have not set yourself.
  • Layout: corner radius and space between days.
  • Color Legend: the scale with its minimum and maximum, at the top or the bottom.
  • Text on a dark or clashing day turns white or dark by itself so it stays readable.

Events and holidays

  • Show events as: Auto (colour marks in month and year, labels in week), Color marks or Labels. Marks keep the heatmap readable; the legend says what each colour is and the tooltip lists the names.
  • Event palette: Vivid (colour-blind safe, the default), Bold, Bright, Dark or Classic — eight clearly different colours each, given to the categories in order of appearance. With Pro each category can also be set by hand.
  • Free shows the first event of each day plus a count of the rest; Pro shows several per day.
  • Click a category in the legend to hide or show its events. It only changes the marks: the value and colour of each day, and the rest of the report, stay as they are. The choice is saved with the report.
  • Legend filters: Only this visual (the default, as above) or The report (bookmarks) — the legend then applies a Power BI filter on Event Category, like a slicer: bookmarks keep it and the other visuals on the page follow it. The calendar itself keeps every day and its value.

Fiscal calendars

Switch on Calendar → Fiscal Calendar and choose the start month. The fiscal year is named after the year in which it ends (a year starting in October 2026 is FY2027).

  • Calendar months: the title adds the fiscal year and period (October 2026 · FY2027 P1) and the year view starts at the fiscal month.
  • 4-4-5, 4-5-4 or 5-4-4 weeks (Fiscal Periods): the year starts on the first day of the week closest to day 1 of the fiscal month, has twelve periods of 4 or 5 whole weeks, and in a 53-week year the extra week goes to period 12. The month view then shows periods, and the year view the twelve periods.

Switching the measure (Pro)

With measures in Tooltip Measures, a Measure selector appears next to the view buttons. The chosen one colours the days and shows its values; the main measure moves to the tooltip. The choice is saved with the report.

Small and narrow visuals

Below 320 px wide or high the visual turns compact: legends and the value of each day step aside and the colour carries the data. Day values also hide when a day is too narrow to read them.

Format pane

In format mode you can also click a day, its number, its value, the view buttons, the legend or the event marks to format them right there.

  • Style template — template, setup guide.
  • Calendar — default view, months to display (Pro), fiscal calendar, start month, fiscal periods, week start, week numbers.
  • Heatmap Colors — color scheme, minimum, maximum, no-data colour, a fixed day colour.
  • Color Rules, Color Legend, Layout, Day Number (font, size, colour, weekend and holiday colours).
  • Events — show, show as, and (Pro) events per day, single colour, text colour and size.
  • (Pro) Measure Labels, View Buttons, Event Category Colors.

The Format pane, the buttons and messages of the visual follow the language of Power BI: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal), Dutch, Polish, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Korean. Day and month names follow the report's locale.

Report integration

  • Tooltips: the date, the value, the holiday, the events and every Tooltip Measure. Report tooltip pages are supported (Format → General → Tooltips); the page receives the hovered day.
  • Number formats: each measure uses its own format string from the model, re-read on every refresh; inside a day the value is abbreviated to fit, tooltips show it in full.

FAQ

Everything falls on one day, or a notice asks for the Day level.

The Date field arrives as a date hierarchy without its Day level. Keep Year, Month and Day in the Date well — the visual rebuilds the dates from them — or switch off File → Options → Current file → Data Load → Auto date/time and add the date column again. Also check in Power Query that the column type is Date.

How do I colour days red below target and green above?

Use Color Rules: rule 1 "greater than or equal to" your target in green, rule 2 "less than or equal to" a lower value in red. The first rule that matches wins.

Why is there no fx button on Day Color any more?

Power BI does not offer conditional formatting to visuals that read data as a table, so the fx in earlier versions never applied. Color Rules do the same job and are free.

How do I filter the report by a range of dates?

Drag across the days, or click the first and Shift+click the last. Ctrl adds to the current selection; clicking empty space clears it.

I chose 3 months and see only 1 or 2.

Months to Display is a maximum: the visual draws the months that fit, at about 200 px each. Widen the visual to see more.

Filtering by event category changes the value of the days.

A slicer on the category removes every row of the other categories, including the rows that hold the day's sales. To filter only the events, click the categories in the visual's legend instead: the marks change and the values do not. Or build the events table with every day × every category (next question): then a slicer keeps every day and its value.

My sales and my events are in two tables.

Relate them by Date (Sales[Date] one to many Events[Date]) and build the events table with every day × every event category, leaving Event Name empty where there is no event. Power BI only sends the visual the days that exist in both tables; with the full grid every day is always there, even when a slicer keeps a single category, so the days keep their sales and only the marks change. Without a filter each day arrives once per category with the same sales: Heatmap Colors → Rows of the same day → Automatic (the default) counts them once.

Does a bookmark remember the categories hidden in the legend?

Set Events → Legend filters to The report (bookmarks). Power BI bookmarks keep filters, selection and visibility, not a visual's settings: in that mode the legend is a filter, so bookmarks keep it (the other visuals on the page are filtered by category too). With Only this visual the choice is saved with the report, like the view and the active measure.

The values inside the days disappeared.

The days are too narrow to read them (or the visual is in compact mode, below 320 px). The colour still carries the data and the tooltip shows the value.

The event names are not written on the days.

Events → Show events as is on Auto: colour marks in month and year, labels in week. Choose Labels to write them in every view.

Two event categories have the same colour.

Up to 8 categories get distinct colours automatically. With Pro you can set each one in Event Category Colors.

How does the 4-4-5 calendar decide when the year starts?

It starts on the first day of the week closest to day 1 of the fiscal start month, so every period has whole weeks. In a 53-week year the extra week goes to period 12.

My template did not change some colours.

A template only fills settings you have not changed yourself. Reset a setting in the Format pane and the template's value applies.

How do I hide the setup guide — or bring it back?

Click ✕ or Finish. It only shows while editing. To bring it back: Format → Style template → Setup guide.

I bought Pro but the Pro features do not appear.

The licence is checked after the calendar first paints, so the free result shows for a moment. If it persists, check you are signed in with the account that holds the licence and that it is assigned in the Microsoft 365 admin center.

Does the visual send my data anywhere?

No. Everything is calculated and drawn inside Power BI and the visual makes no network calls of any kind. See the Privacy Policy.

Does it work in Power BI Service and mobile?

Yes: Desktop, Service, Embedded and the mobile apps.
